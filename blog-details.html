Dance Education

Why Classical Dance is the Best Foundation for Every Child's Arts Journey

Priya Ramaswamy
Classical Dance Director, Artiste Academy
November 20, 2025
8 min read
2,840 views
"In thirty years of teaching, I have never seen a discipline that transforms children quite the way classical dance does — not just as performers, but as human beings."

When parents bring their children to us for the first time, they often ask: "Should we start with something modern? Won't classical dance be too strict for a five-year-old?" The question comes from a place of love — every parent wants their child to enjoy the experience. But what most parents don't yet know is that classical Indian dance is, paradoxically, one of the most joyful and developmentally rich activities a young child can undertake.

The Language of the Body

Classical dance forms like Bharatanatyam are built on Abhinaya — the art of expression. Unlike many physical activities that focus solely on technique, classical dance from the very first lesson teaches children to communicate stories through their body. A four-year-old learning to portray a peacock in the rain isn't just memorizing a movement — she's developing empathy, imagination, and the ability to inhabit a character.

"When my daughter learned the opening sequence of Bharatanatyam, she didn't just learn a dance. She learned how to speak without words." — Meena Krishnaswamy, Artiste Academy parent

Seven Profound Benefits We've Observed

Across 20 years and over 500 students, our faculty has documented remarkable transformations in children who study classical dance. Here are the seven most consistent benefits:

  • Posture and body alignment: The foundational stances (Aramandi, Muzhumandi) develop core strength and spinal alignment that benefit children in every physical activity.
  • Rhythmic intelligence: The intricate footwork patterns (Nritta) build mathematical thinking alongside musical sensitivity. Many of our students excel in mathematics as a result.
  • Memory and concentration: Learning complex sequences of movements and expressions requires and develops remarkable powers of memorization and focus.
  • Emotional intelligence: Portraying the nine Navarasa (emotions) helps children identify, understand, and constructively express their own emotions.
  • Cultural connection: Understanding the stories, myths and values embedded in classical forms gives children a deep pride in their cultural heritage.
  • Performance confidence: The structured journey from classroom to stage builds a kind of calm, grounded confidence that stays with children for life.
  • Artistic foundation: Children who study classical dance pick up other art forms — ballet, western dance, music — with remarkable ease and depth of understanding.

What About "Strictness"?

The concern about strictness is worth addressing directly. Yes, classical dance has rules — precise positions, specific rhythms, formal structures. But in the hands of a skilled teacher, these rules become not a cage but a scaffold. Children feel safe within structure. They know exactly what they're working toward. And the moment a child executes a technically correct movement with genuine expression — that moment of pride is one of the most powerful experiences in a young person's life.

At Artiste Academy, we are careful to distinguish between discipline (which we celebrate) and rigidity (which we reject). Our youngest students learn through play, story and imagination. The technical requirements come gradually, always in service of expression rather than instead of it.

Starting Early vs. Starting Right

Parents often wonder about the "right" age to begin. Our experience is clear: three years old is not too young for introduction to classical dance — but only if the approach is age-appropriate. Our Tiny Tots program (ages 3–5) uses storytelling, songs and imaginative play to introduce the Mudras (hand gestures) and basic footwork rhythms. By the time these children graduate to structured classes at age 6, they have a body vocabulary that their peers will spend years developing.

A Word to Skeptical Parents

If you're still unsure, we invite you to simply come and watch. Book a free studio visit — sit at the back of one of our Juniors classes and observe. Watch what happens when a seven-year-old loses herself completely in the expression of a story through dance. Watch the light in her eyes. Then imagine that child in ten years — the poise, the confidence, the artistry.

That is what classical dance gives. Not just a skill. A way of being in the world.
